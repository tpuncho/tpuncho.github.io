LIFE — ARTICLE 02

When Your Best Friend Doesn't Feel Like Your Best Friend Anymore.

Sometimes nobody does anything wrong. You just wake up one day and realize things aren't the same.

THE IN-BETWEEN ISSUE 01 / LIFE

There’s a weird kind of sadness that comes with realizing you don't talk to someone the way you used to.

Not because you had a huge fight.

Not because someone betrayed someone.

Not because there was some dramatic ending where you both decided you weren't friends anymore.

Sometimes nothing happens.

You just slowly stop being as close.

And honestly, that can feel worse.

Because when there's a reason, at least you know what you're supposed to be upset about.

But when there isn't one?

You're just left wondering when everything changed.

You used to tell them everything.

The random things.

The embarrassing things.

The things that weren't even worth telling anyone else.

You'd send them a picture of something stupid you saw on the way home.

You'd text them the second something happened.

You'd know what they were doing without even asking.

And then, slowly, you don't.

You still talk.

You still like each other's posts.

You still say hi at school.

But something feels different.

Maybe you don't text first anymore.

Maybe they don't either.

Maybe your conversations are mostly:

“Are you coming tomorrow?”

“Did you do the homework?”

“Wait what did she say?”

And that's it.

Nothing is technically wrong.

But you miss when there was more.

Sometimes the friendship didn't end. It just changed.

I think that's one of the hardest things about getting older.

We talk about friendships ending like there's always a clear moment when it happens.

But sometimes there isn't.

Sometimes you're still friends.

You just aren't those friends anymore.

The ones who stayed up until 2 a.m. talking.

The ones who had inside jokes nobody else understood.

The ones who could tell something was wrong just by the way the other person said “I'm fine.”

You don't necessarily stop caring about them.

You just become different people.

And sometimes different people don't fit together the same way they used to.

Not every friendship needs a villain for it to change.

And then you start wondering if it's your fault.

Maybe I should text them more.

Maybe I was being distant.

Maybe they think I don't care.

Maybe I should've tried harder.

Maybe I ruined it.

You replay old conversations.

You look at old pictures.

You scroll through messages from two years ago and suddenly you're nostalgic for a version of your life that doesn't exist anymore.

And that's when it gets complicated.

Because you don't just miss the person.

You miss who you were with them.

Growing up changes your friendships too.

New classes.

New friends.

New interests.

Different schedules.

Different priorities.

Someone gets a job.

Someone starts dating.

Someone gets really into something you don't care about.

Someone becomes closer with another person.

And suddenly your friendship isn't the centre of both of your lives anymore.

Which is normal.

But normal doesn't always mean it doesn't hurt.

Sometimes you can be genuinely happy for someone and still miss them.

Both things can be true.

You don't always have to fix it.

This is probably the part nobody wants to hear.

Sometimes you can try.

Text them.

Ask to hang out.

Actually talk about what's been bothering you.

Sometimes the friendship comes back.

Sometimes it doesn't.

And sometimes you realize you don't actually want the old friendship back.

You just want the feeling you had when it existed.

There's a difference.

You can appreciate what someone meant to you without forcing them to stay in your life forever.

And you can let a friendship change without pretending it never mattered.

Maybe growing up means making room.

For new people.

For old people in new forms.

For friendships that last.

For friendships that don't.

For people you thought you'd know forever but eventually only see once in a while.

For the friend you haven't met yet.

Maybe not every person is supposed to come with you through every version of your life.

Some people are there for one chapter.

Some stay for five.

Some somehow make it through all of them.

You don't always get to know which one someone will be.

And maybe that's okay.

Because the fact that something changed doesn't mean it wasn't real.

It just means you're changing too.

And honestly?

That's probably one of the hardest parts of growing up.

You don't just have to figure out who you are.

You also have to figure out who still fits into your life.
