LIFE — COVER STORY

Everyone Is
Figuring It Out.

School. Friendships. What comes next. Somehow everyone expects us to have a plan before we've even figured out who we are.

THE IN-BETWEEN ISSUE 01 / LIFE

There’s a point in high school where the questions start getting a little too serious.

“What are you doing after graduation?”

“What do you want to study?”

“Where do you see yourself in five years?”

Five years?

I’m still deciding what I’m wearing tomorrow.

And somehow, everyone expects an answer.

You can be sitting in class half-listening to your teacher while thinking about what you're doing after school, and suddenly someone is talking about university applications, scholarships, careers, moving out, or studying in another country.

Then you open Instagram and someone you went to elementary school with is posting about their acceptance letter.

Someone else is travelling.

Someone got a job.

Someone is suddenly in love.

Someone looks like they have their entire life planned.

And you're just sitting there thinking:

Wait. Was I supposed to be doing something?

Maybe everyone just looks like they know what they're doing.

The weird thing is that most people don't actually look confused.

They have the outfits. The plans. The friend groups. The playlists. The perfectly timed photo dumps.

They know where they're going Friday night.

They know what university they want.

They know what they're wearing to the party.

They somehow know what they want to do with their entire life.

At least, that's what it looks like.

But you never really know what's happening outside the frame.

The girl who seems completely confident might go home and question every conversation she had that day.

The person who seems like they have their future planned might change their mind next year.

The friend who always knows exactly what to say might have no idea what they're doing either.

Maybe we're all just really good at looking like we aren't figuring it out.

You can look like you have it together and still have absolutely no idea what you're doing.

Growing up is basically a collection of tiny goodbyes.

Nobody really tells you this part.

Sometimes growing up means losing people you thought you'd know forever.

Sometimes it means realizing that a friendship doesn't feel the same anymore.

Sometimes your friend group changes without anyone officially saying that it changed.

One day you're talking every day.

Then you're not.

Then you see each other at school and it's not exactly awkward, but it's not the same either.

And nobody did anything wrong.

You just changed.

That's one of the strangest things about getting older.

You can love someone and still grow in completely different directions.

You can miss an old version of your life without actually wanting it back.

And you can be excited about what's next while still feeling a little sad about what you're leaving behind.

And then there's the future.

The future is weird because everyone talks about it like it's a place you're supposed to arrive at.

Graduate.

Go to university.

Get a job.

Move out.

Figure out your career.

Have your life together.

But what if your life doesn't happen in that order?

What if you change your mind?

What if you go somewhere and realize you don't want to stay?

What if the thing you thought you'd love ends up being completely wrong for you?

What if you don't know yet?

Honestly, maybe that's okay.

Maybe you don't need a five-year plan. Maybe you just need a next step.

Take the class you're curious about.

Apply for the volunteer position.

Get the part-time job.

Go to the event even if you don't know anyone.

Talk to someone new.

Try something and hate it.

Try something and love it.

Change your mind.

Start over.

Do something just because you're curious.

Not everything needs to become part of your “future.”

Sometimes it can just be something you wanted to try.

We're all making it up as we go.

Maybe that's what nobody tells you.

There isn't actually a moment where you suddenly become an adult and everything makes sense.

You don't wake up one morning with the perfect career, perfect friendships, perfect routine, perfect apartment, perfect skin, perfect bank account and a perfectly organized calendar.

You just keep figuring things out.

One decision at a time.

One awkward conversation.

One new friendship.

One bad grade.

One good day.

One random Tuesday night where you suddenly realize you're actually excited about something.

And maybe that's what growing up is.

Not becoming someone who has everything figured out.

Just becoming someone who's okay with figuring it out.

Because honestly?

Everyone is figuring it out.

Some people are just better at making it look like they're not.
